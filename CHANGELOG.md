# Changelog

- Controls: `param{name,value,min,max,about}` in a script shows as a slider (Universe tab) and can be changed live; rules read it by name. Experiments can change controls for the second run without editing code.
- New examples: forest fire (physics + chemistry), reaction-diffusion patterns.
- Roles: controls have `owner`s; "Play as" locks everything but your role's controls, and experiments respect it.
- Shared limits: `gsum/gcount/gmean/gmax/gmin(kind, prop)` read whole-universe totals in rules, so water and money can run out.
- **3D view.** Tick "3D" in the Universe tab: orbit camera (drag, scroll, follow a thing), a light, ground and grid, and shapes per thing (sphere, cube, pyramid, or any `.obj`). The camera and light belong to the universe (`camera{}`, `light{}`) and are saved. A saved universe is a project folder with `script.luau` and a `models/` folder for your `.obj` files. New example: Bouncing things (3D).

## Unreleased

- **Real smoke and water (new `simtara-fields` crate).** Fluids in metres and seconds that any Build universe can use from its script. Smoke: stable fluids on a staggered grid (MacCormack advection, buoyancy of hot air, heavy smoke sinking, vorticity confinement, red-black SOR pressure solve), wind tunnels with inflow and open sides, solids (`box`, `ball`, `cylinder`). Water: FLIP particles on the same grid, with taps (`emitter{water = ...}`). Commands: `fluid{...}`, `solid{...}`, `emitter{...}`, `water{...}`, `fluid_metric{...}`, `fluid_info{}`; any number may name a `param` slider. Runs repeat exactly. Tests check that air stays incompressible, hot smoke rises, heavy smoke sinks, wind carries smoke, still water stays still and level, and a dam break is no faster than the shallow-water limit 2√(gh).
- **Smoke drawn as smoke.** The 3D view ray-marches smoke, fire glow and solids, on the GPU (full resolution) or the CPU (lower resolution); a test checks both give the same picture. Water is drawn as drops, blue to white where it moves fast.
- New examples: smoke and wind around a building, a fire plume, a dam break.
- **Real liquids.** `fluid{liquid = "honey"}` sets real viscosity and density from standard tables (water, sea water, milk, mercury, olive oil, glycerine, honey, lava); `viscosity` and `density` can also be given, viscosity can follow a slider. Viscosity is solved on the grid with no-slip walls. `solid{cup = {...}}` makes an open glass; `emitter{water_ml = ...}` pours in millilitres per second. New example: pour a real liquid into a glass. Tests: honey runs out far slower than water and olive oil, mass = volume x density, a glass holds what is poured in.
- **Water made correct (APIC, volume, ghost-fluid surface, conjugate gradients).** Liquids now use APIC (Jiang et al. 2015) instead of FLIP: no more noisy spray, splashes stay coherent. The pressure solve keeps the volume (crowded cells pushed apart, thinned-out cells pulled back to the rest density, plus particle separation), places the free surface between cell centres (ghost fluid, Gibou et al. 2002) and is solved with preconditioned conjugate gradients. Before, the dam break's water grew by ~26%; now it stays within 5%. Validation tests: still water's pressure equals rho*g*depth (within 8%), and a tank sloshes at the period linear wave theory gives (0.81 s vs 0.87 s). Spray is white only where droplets fly on their own. `fluid_metric` can read `pressure` in pascals; new measurements `water.filled_m3` and `water.centre_x_m`. The `flip` setting is no longer used.
- Dam-break example: `DETAIL` at the top of the script, `fast` (4 cm cells, default, closer to real time) or `fine` (2.5 cm, sharper, slower).
- **Smooth and light at 60 fps.** What the 3D view draws for a fluid (smoke/solids picture, liquid field, spray, caustics) is now made once per step on the fluid's own thread, not every frame, and goes to the GPU only when it changed. Caustics are computed in parallel (69 ms to a few ms). The water's ray march strides through empty space and the solids' march refines with bisection; a 1600x900 water frame takes 2-6 ms on the GPU. Between steps the water blends from the last step's field to the newest, so it moves smoothly at the window's frame rate even when the simulation steps less often. Fewer pressure sweeps on the GPU (3 per iteration, all tests still pass): the dam break steps in about 75 ms instead of 120. The caption shows fps.
- **Water is one continuous surface, never particles.** The particles now only build a smooth liquid field (quadratic B-spline, `liquid::surface_field`); the GPU follows each pixel's ray into it and finds the exact surface (bisection), with normals from the field's gradient, then refracts through it to measure the water crossed. No spheres or circles at any distance. Replaces the screen-space sphere splatting.
- **Water scenes drawn like Evan Wallace's WebGL Water** (studied via jeantimex/webgpu-water): liquid scenes get a sky to reflect, the tank drawn as a tiled pool (far walls and floor only, soft corner shadows), the water refracting what is behind it by Snell's law (the scene is drawn first without water, then looked up along the bent ray), Fresnel reflection of the sky and sun, absorption by depth, and caustics on the floor: sunlight refracted through the simulated surface and focused into bright lines (`Fluid::caustics`, computed from the water's column heights). Solids in liquid scenes are drawn with depth so water can be in front of them.
- **Spray as mist.** Droplets with little water around them are drawn as several soft, see-through white puffs each (GPU), in the spirit of the spray/foam classification of Ihmsen et al. 2012, instead of balls. Thin isolated bits of water count as spray too.
- **A real surface mesh.** `simtara_fields::mesh` builds a closed, watertight triangle mesh of a liquid from its particles: Zhu & Bridson (2005) distance field on a grid twice as fine as the simulation, marching tetrahedra, Taubin smoothing, outward normals; closed off at tank walls. Export: the 💧 button in the 3D view (`Documents/SimTara/exports/<name>-water-<tick>.obj`) or `simtara-studio --picture FILE --ticks 30,60 --out frame --obj`. OBJ is in metres, z up (Blender: Up Z, Forward Y). Tests: a ball comes out round, closed and within 8% of its volume, still water's top is flat and at the right height, water filling a tank keeps its volume; the dam break's mesh holds 0.60-0.62 m³, matching the water.
- **Faster GPU read back.** Only packed particles, pressure and one number come back each tick, through a staging buffer kept between ticks; face velocities stay on the card and liquid speeds are measured from particles. Read back went from about 34 to 10 ms, a tick at 605,000 particles from 101 to 68 ms.
- **Liquids solved on the GPU.** New `simtara-fields` GPU path (wgpu compute, `gpu_liquid.wgsl`): the same APIC / volume-correction / ghost-fluid / red-black SOR steps as the CPU, one thread per particle, face or cell, with fixed-point sums so runs repeat exactly on the same GPU. Particles stay on the card between ticks. Used automatically when a graphics card is there (`fluid{backend = "cpu"}` forces the CPU); falls back to the CPU otherwise. A test checks GPU and CPU agree (volume within 4%, height and position within 8%) and that the GPU repeats exactly. The dam-break example now runs at 2.5 cm cells with about 300,000 particles (about 120 ms per tick). Only droplets with almost nothing around them are drawn as spray.
- **Water looks like water.** On the GPU, liquid particles are no longer drawn as balls: they are splatted into a depth picture, smoothed with a bilateral filter so they melt into one surface (screen-space fluid rendering, van der Laan et al. 2009), and shaded with Fresnel reflection of the sky, a sun highlight and light absorbed by the depth of water it passes through. Only real spray (fast droplets flying alone) is drawn as small white drops. The CPU painter still draws drops. A test checks the surface is smooth and water-coloured.
- **Pictures without a window.** `simtara-studio --picture FILE.luau --ticks 30,80 --out frame --size 960x600` saves what the 3D view shows as PNGs.
- **Smooth while fluids compute.** A fluid's next step is computed on another thread; Studio keeps drawing (and the camera keeps turning) while it runs, and skips the tick until it is ready. The step shown is always exactly one behind, so runs still repeat exactly, also across a save; a test checks this.
- **3D view on the GPU, with the CPU as fallback.** The Universe tab's 3D view now draws on the graphics card (wgpu, the same device as the window): real depth, per-pixel lit balls, instanced cubes, pyramids and `.obj` models, 4x anti-aliasing. A **GPU** checkbox next to the camera switches back to the CPU painter, which is also used when there is no graphics card. The caption shows which one is drawing and how long it takes.
- **Airflow example (3D).** A wind tunnel of 6,048 air cells around a building (or two buildings, or a ball): air carries itself and smoke, viscosity, pressure pushes air around the obstacle. Controls for wind, viscosity, gusts and smoke; probes measure the pressure on the front and the wind behind it. A simplified model for seeing flow, not engineering numbers.

- **Build screen in Studio.** "Build your own" template: Luau editor (colours, line numbers, errors at the line), live charts of your own measurements, a table of every thing with editable values, a rules panel (toggle, errors, typo warnings), a command reference with Insert buttons, examples (empty, idea spreads, typed Luau, epidemic), and a **Build & test** tab with checks like `people who know > 100`.
- **Build and test from the command line.** `simtara script FILE --ticks N --expect "metric >= 10"` prints a JSON report (errors, measurements over time, things, rules, warnings, checks, repeatability) and exits 1 on failure. `simtara describe` lists everything a script can use, for coding agents.
- **Things and rules (`simtara-things`).** Domain-neutral things with properties and relations, script-defined measurements, `setup()`, and native rules (formulas run in parallel on every thing of a kind each tick; 100,000 things x 5 ticks about 120 ms, repeatable). A `warnings` command flags properties a rule reads that nothing sets.
- **Scripting moved from Lua 5.4 to Luau** (typed, built-in sandbox).
- **Orbital gravity.** New `simtara-orbits` crate: Sun, Earth and Moon in real SI units, leapfrog integration, energy conserved. One tick = 6 hours, so 1461 ticks = one year. Studio gets a "Sun, Earth & Moon" template with follow/zoom view and trails.
- **Event scripting (Lua, now Luau).** New `simtara-script` crate: a sandboxed Lua script saved with the universe, `on_tick(t)` runs every tick (for example `remove_body("Moon")`). No files, network or randomness; runaway loops are stopped and reported. Modules expose actions through a new generic command registry in core (`World::register_command` / `call`).
- **Script experiments.** An experiment can now change the script instead of a law ("What happens if the Moon disappears?"), and compare any measurement of the two runs.
- **Save and reopen universes.** New `simtara-store` crate stores a universe as a folder (`universe.json` + `state.snap.json`, with a `schema_version`) under `Documents/SimTara/universes/` (override with `SIMTARA_HOME`). Studio launcher gets a "My universes" list (open, delete) and a Save button; leaving a saved universe autosaves it. `simtara-studio --open "<name>"` skips the launcher.
- **Experiments.** In the Studio, change one law (gravity, friction, range, core repel, charge), run the original and the changed version from the same moment on CPU, and see a two-line chart. Results are saved with the universe (`experiment.json`, `results.json`, the exact `start.snap.json`, and a run record: engine version, seed, backend, time) and shown again on reopen.
- `serde_json` now uses `float_roundtrip`, so saved numbers reload bit-for-bit (found by a test: results changed in the last digit after a save).
- **License changed from MIT to Elastic License 2.0** (no hosted/managed-service use). v0.1.0 and v0.2.0 remain MIT. Added ENTERPRISE.md describing the planned commercial tier.

## v0.2.0

- New `simtara-studio` app: launcher with parameter sliders, then live universe with control panel (play/pause, live laws, particle count, CPU/GPU, metrics graph).

- New `simtara-api` crate: JSON control interface (create/run/observe/configure/branch/snapshot worlds) for CLI, Studio and AI agents. `simtara serve` speaks it as JSON lines over stdin/stdout.
- Core: metric registry (`Builder::add_metric`, `Simulation::collect_metrics`); particles register `particles.count`, `particles.kinetic_energy`, `particles.mean_neighbors`.
- New `simtara-gpu` crate: wgpu + WGSL particle force kernel, registered as `Backend::Gpu`; automatic CPU fallback.
- Core: `Builder.compute` (modules can register backends), `Compute::active()`.
- Particles: shared `build_grid` / `accelerations_cpu` / `apply_acceleration` so CPU and GPU share one contract.
- CLI: `--backend gpu|cpu`, `G` key to switch live, ms/tick reporting.

## v0.1.0

First release.

- `simtara-core`: domain-neutral runtime — world, entities/components, ticks, staged systems,
  events, config, snapshots, module interface, compute-backend selection (CPU only for now).
- `simtara-particles`: particle module with attraction/repulsion, friction, bounce/wrap boundaries,
  live-tunable parameters.
- `simtara-cli`: `simtara run` (headless, save/load/PNG) and `simtara view` (live window).

Not yet implemented in v0.1.0: GPU backend, slider UI (both added in v0.2.0).
